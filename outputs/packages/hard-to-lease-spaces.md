## The argument
- Every multi-tenant property has spaces that are easier to lease and spaces that are harder.
- Retail: the elbow space (the inside corner where two wings of an L-shaped center meet). Office: the suite facing the parking garage or with no view.
- The mistake: owners expect the same thing from every space, desirable or not.
- Hard spaces need creativity. If you won't get creative, it stays vacant.
- How: figure out who realistically leases that space, find their hot buttons and motivations, and within reason, get it done.
- The vacancy hurts more than whatever it costs to get creative.

## YouTube description
In every multi-tenant property, some spaces are easier to lease than others. In a retail center, it's the elbow space, the inside corner where two wings of the center meet. In an office building, it's the suite that looks out at the parking garage or doesn't have a good view. A lot of owners have the same expectations for every space in the building, whether it's a desirable space or not.

When you're trying to fill one of those harder spaces, you have to get creative. If you're not willing to, that space is probably going to keep sitting vacant. What creative looks like depends on the property. Start by understanding who could realistically lease that space. When someone shows interest, figure out their hot buttons and their motivations, and within reason, get the deal done.

The vacancy is going to hurt you a lot more than getting creative to lease a really tough space.
