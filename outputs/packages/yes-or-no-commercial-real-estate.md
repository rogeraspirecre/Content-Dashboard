## EDIT NOTES
- **Cold open on the office apocalypse answer,** even though it's the last one filmed and the least yes-or-no. It's the only item a non-industry viewer already has an opinion on.
- **"What for?" is the best human beat in the set.** He answers the residential agent question with a question. Keep the pause.
- **Format drift:** the office apocalypse answer is two full sentences, breaking the one-word rule. Worth it here because it's the payoff, but it's the reason it should lead or close, not sit in the middle.
- **Houston verdict is now on record: "some things are, but for the most part, no."** That's the thesis for the "Is Houston Overbuilt?" sequel seeded from the overbuilt explainer. The full video needs to answer the obvious follow-up: which things are.
- **Office apocalypse is its own full video.** "Most expensive buildings doing better than ever, cheap ones worse than ever" is the flight-to-quality story, and Brandon has the receipt for it already: Williams Tower going from $412M to $300M. A named Houston building plus the split verdict is a top-of-funnel video, not a niche one.
- **"Yes, buy an office building" vs Set 10's "own your office: 3."** These aren't the same question: one is buying a building as an investment, the other is a business owning the space it operates from. Posted near each other, some viewers will read it as a contradiction. The Set 10 flag about the condo ads still needs a decision either way.
