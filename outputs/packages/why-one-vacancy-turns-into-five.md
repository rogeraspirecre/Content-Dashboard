## YouTube description
Vacancy is more dangerous than most owners realize, because it doesn't just cost you the rent you're not collecting. It slowly changes how the market sees the property.

When there's one vacancy or two at a property, it's no big deal. The general assumption is natural turnover, and that the space will be filled soon. But when one or two vacancies turns into four or five or six, and the property is half occupied or less, the market senses that something is wrong. And when people sense something is wrong, they assume it's because the owner or the property manager isn't doing a good job, that tenants' needs aren't being met, that repairs aren't being made, and that nobody is reinvesting in the property.

That's why when you get a vacancy, you fill it as quickly as possible. You don't know what may happen. Tenants go bankrupt all the time and unexpected things come up. Every time you have one or two vacancies, aggressively work to get them leased, so when the next one or two happen unexpectedly, you don't have a property that looks mostly vacant. That sends the wrong message to the market and makes those vacancies much harder to lease.
