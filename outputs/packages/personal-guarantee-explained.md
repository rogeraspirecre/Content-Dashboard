## The argument
- A commercial lease is usually signed by the tenant's LLC or operating entity, not the owner personally.
- A personal guarantee means the owner guarantees the landlord that if the business goes under or shuts down, they're personally responsible for the lease payments no matter what.
- Tenants don't want one. Landlords always want one. It comes down to risk.
- New business, no financial or operational history: you're signing one, because you haven't proven you can be trusted to pay rent.
- Ten years in business, millions in revenue, long track record: you shouldn't have to sign one. If you did, something unusual is going on or the deal wasn't negotiated properly.
- Controversial and important for both sides. For small businesses there's really no difference between the business and the person, and tenants walk their leases all the time, so without a track record it's dangerous for a landlord to go without one.

## YouTube description
When a tenant signs a commercial lease, it's usually their LLC or whatever entity they run the business through that signs, not the business owner personally. A personal guarantee is when the business owner guarantees to the building owner that if the business goes under or shuts down, they're personally responsible for making those lease payments no matter what.

Obviously tenants don't want a personal guarantee, and landlords always want one. It comes down to risk. If you're a new business with no financial history and no operational history, you're going to have to sign one, because you haven't proven you can be trusted to pay rent. If you've owned a business for ten years, it makes millions of dollars a year, and you have a long track record, you shouldn't have to sign one. If you did, either something unusual is going on or the deal wasn't negotiated properly.

It's a controversial point and an important one for both sides. The reality is that for small businesses, there's really no difference between the business and the person. Tenants walk away from their leases all the time, so unless a tenant has a long track record, it's dangerous for a landlord not to have a personal guarantee.
