## YouTube description
What's my biggest career regret? I don't really believe in regrets. I think you should learn from your mistakes, focus on the future, and not dwell on the past. But I definitely wish I had enjoyed the struggle more.

When you're trying to build a business or a career, it's easy to get caught up in the stress and all the negative things you have to go through along the way. But those negative things, the failures and the setbacks, are what let me grow. If I hadn't gone through those painful stretches, I wouldn't have learned what I needed to learn to reach my goals and stay on the path I'm on.

The difficult times are preparing you for who you want to be.

## EDIT NOTES
- **Open on the answer, not the disclaimer.** "I don't believe in regrets" is the most common non-answer to this question. "I wish I would have enjoyed the struggle more" is the real answer. Lead with it.
- **Last line leans generic.** "The difficult times are a blessing that are preparing you for who you want to be" is true but sounds like a quote card. If the cut runs long, end on "if I didn't go through those painful times, I wouldn't have learned what I needed to learn." That's his and it's specific.
- **Same theme as the skill clip.** "The Skill That's Made Me the Most Money" is about getting more aggressive after a loss; this one is about appreciating the losses in hindsight. Either space them out or post them as a pair on consecutive days, so they read as a mindset series rather than a repeat.
- **Needs the same thing the skill clip needs: a real example.** Which struggle does he wish he'd enjoyed? One named hard stretch (first year broke, the deal that fell apart, starting Aspire) would make this memorable. Add it to the next-shoot questions alongside the loss story.
- **Broad lane.** No real estate knowledge needed. Judge it against the Q&A and aspirational videos.
