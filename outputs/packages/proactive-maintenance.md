## The argument
- Fix it before the tenant complains. That's the cheap version.
- Proactive maintenance on the building and systems is always cheaper over the long run.
- It is **not** cheaper in the next 30, 60, 90 days. He says this out loud, which is what makes it credible.
- Wait until it breaks and you get two bills: the emergency repair, and a tenant who's less happy and more likely to leave.
- The turnover cost is the real cost. That's the part owners don't price in.

## YouTube description
The cheapest way to fix a maintenance issue is to fix it before your tenant is calling to complain about it.

Being proactive about maintenance, taking care of the building and the systems before something breaks, is always going to be cheaper over the long run. It won't look cheaper in the next 30, 60, or 90 days, and that's why most owners don't do it. But when you wait until things break and it becomes an emergency, it costs more to fix and your tenants are less happy and more likely to leave.

That second part is the cost nobody puts on the spreadsheet. Losing a good tenant over something you could have handled early is far more expensive than the repair would have been.
