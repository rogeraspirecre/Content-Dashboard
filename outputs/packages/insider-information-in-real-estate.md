One of the main things that separates good real estate investors from bad ones is information. Good investors have data you can't look up on the internet or get from ChatGPT. Bad investors make decisions based on information anyone can access.

Every market is different. You have to understand what's going on in that area: what's happened over the years, what's changed, and where it's going. Which tenants moved in, and what were the terms of those deals? Which landlords are doing well, and which ones aren't?

What's funny is that if you use nonpublic information to buy stocks, you go to jail. In real estate, making decisions with information that isn't public is completely legal. And if you don't have that kind of information, you're not going to get very far.

## EDIT NOTES
- **The key line is garbled as spoken.** He says "when you're buying stocks, you go to jail" and then "if you use nonpublic information to make decisions in real estate, it's completely legal." The sentence splits in the wrong place, so on first listen it sounds like buying stocks gets you jailed. Fix with on-screen text: "Nonpublic info in stocks = jail. In real estate = legal." Or grab a clean pickup: "Use nonpublic information to buy stocks and you go to jail. In real estate, it's completely legal."
- **This has broad reach potential.** Insider trading is a concept everyone recognizes (Finding 7), so this is closer to top-of-funnel than a typical investor clip. Treat it as a reach test, not an owner-education post.
- **Receipt that fits:** Texas is a non-disclosure state, so sale prices aren't public record. That's the strongest proof of his point and it's local. Worth a text overlay or a follow-up clip: "In Texas, you can't even look up what a building sold for."
- **Keep the wording precise in captions and titles.** His point is about market knowledge gathered legitimately (deal terms, tenant moves, landlord health). It isn't about using a client's confidential information, which brokers are bound to protect, or about publicly traded real estate like REITs, which are securities. The package copy stays on the safe side of that line. Don't write any caption that implies Aspire trades on what clients tell them.
- **ChatGPT line is timely.** "You can't look up on the internet or ask ChatGPT" dates it to now in a good way. Keep it.
- **Pairs with the receipts content.** "We close 20+ deals a month" is exactly where this information comes from. A follow-up that connects the two ("this is why deal volume matters") turns an abstract point into Aspire's advantage.
