## YouTube description
As a building owner, the work to renew a tenant starts the day they sign their first lease. It doesn't matter when the lease expires. Every day a tenant is in your property, they're evaluating it and forming opinions about it.

If they're happy, if you're responsive, if you're flexible, and if you treat the relationship as a partnership instead of someone who's there to pay you money, they're much more likely to renew than leave.

There's nothing more expensive than backfilling space. Your best tenant is almost always your current tenant, and any chance you have to keep them, you should take. That process starts now, not when their lease is up.

## EDIT NOTES
- **"Nothing more expensive than backfilling space" needs a receipt.** It's the strongest claim in the clip and it's unsupported on camera. A quick overlay of what a backfill actually costs (months of downtime, free rent, TI, leasing commissions) would make it land. Get the real breakdown from Brandon for a typical Houston suite; don't estimate it.
- **Watch the "best tenant" phrasing.** Set 6 says "the best tenants are profitable businesses," and this clip says "your best tenant is your current tenant." They don't conflict, but same wording two different ways. Don't post them in the same week.
- **This is the fifth piece in the proactive frame** (marketing clip, vacancies checklist, proactive maintenance, chasing broker updates, now this). All five say the same thing: the owner who waits pays more. That's enough for a YouTube playlist, something like "The Owner Playbook," and each clip should point to the next in its end screen.
- **Reach expectation:** owner education, low organic reach. Pairs well with the proactive maintenance clip, which ends on the same risk: unhappy tenants leave.
