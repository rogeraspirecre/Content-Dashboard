## YouTube description
You've heard the old saying that there's no such thing as a free lunch. It's true, and it applies to free rent. Technically you're getting some months free at the beginning of a lease, but here's why the landlord is doing it and why it isn't really free.

The value of a building is based on its net operating income. The higher the NOI, the higher the value. Say there's a space where a tenant would be willing to pay $90,000 a year plus triple net expenses. If the tenant pays $90,000 a year and the building sells at an 8% cap rate, the building is worth $1,125,000. Now say the landlord can charge $100,000 a year instead by giving you a bunch of free rent up front. Once that free rent expires and you're paying $100,000 a year, at an 8 cap the building is worth $1,250,000.

That's $125,000 more value, because the landlord gave you free rent up front and got you to pay a higher rate. And tenants usually account for their lease on a straight line basis anyway, so they're taking the average across the term. Free rent is more financial engineering than it is truly free rent.
