## The tactic
1. Find properties listed by agents with no experience in that property type (residential realtor listing industrial, etc.)
2. They misprice it — usually WAY too high
3. But the owner hired an agent, which means they want to sell
4. Owner gives it time at the recommended price
5. Eventually reality forces the price down: 1 month, 6 months, 2 years
6. Everyone else saw the price and moved on permanently
7. You followed up. That's the deal.

## YouTube description
In this video, I share a strategy for finding great commercial properties to buy: look for listings handled by agents who don't have experience with that property type.

Think about a residential realtor listing an industrial building, or someone who's never listed retail marketing a retail property for sale. They don't have the expertise, so they don't know how to price it.

Before you get excited, they usually price it way too high. Every once in a while it's low, but usually it's high. Here's why that still matters. The fact that the owner hired an agent at all means they want to sell. So they'll give it time at whatever price that agent recommended.

Eventually, if they really want to sell, the price has to come down to reality. It might take a month. It might take six months. It might take two years. Everyone else who reached out, saw that price, and moved on never followed up again.

That's the opportunity. Stay in touch, keep following up, and be patient. That's how you find great deals.
