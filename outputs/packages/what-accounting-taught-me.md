## YouTube description
What did accounting teach me? The importance of enjoying what you do.

When I was in public accounting, I was forced to work 15 plus hour days. Sometimes it was necessary, and sometimes you did it because that's the culture. I didn't enjoy it. I work more throughout the year now than I ever did in accounting, but back then I couldn't stand those hours, because I didn't enjoy the work. Now I enjoy what I do, and it's a privilege to work those hours.

On the technical side, accounting forces you to get in the weeds and understand the numbers, and that gives me an advantage in real estate. A lot of people who sell or lease real estate are great salespeople but don't have the technical side. I have the technical side from my accounting background, and I've learned how to sell over the years too.

- **Two clips in one.** The enjoyment half is broad career content. The technical-advantage half is real estate positioning. If you want reach, cut them into two posts and let the hours version carry the wider audience.
- **The technical half is a positioning line for Aspire.** "Great salespeople who don't have the technical side" is a real differentiator and it's the kind of thing that belongs on the website and in the pitch deck, not just in a post.
- **Consistent with the record.** He graded accounting and the CPA an 8 in Degrees by ROI Pt 2 and defended it against the AI narrative. This clip explains why he rates it that high. Worth linking them in an end screen.
- **It could use one number.** "15 plus hours a day" is the concrete detail that works. If he has a second one (how long he stayed, what year he left, what he made), it would make the backstory stick.
- **Personal brand lane.** He has relatively few origin-story pieces. This one covers the career switch and the reason he's credible on numbers, so it's a good pinned or profile candidate alongside the talked-a-client-out-of-a-deal clip.
