## The argument
- Buy a commercial building and the IRS makes you depreciate it over 39 years.
- But a building is made of parts that don't last 39 years: HVAC, parking lot striping, landscaping, flooring, cabinets.
- A cost segregation study is a report you hire a firm to produce. It breaks the building into components and sorts them into categories with shorter depreciation lives.
- 100% bonus depreciation lets you write off everything with a life shorter than 39 years in year one.
- The 2025 bill made 100% bonus depreciation permanent. The 2017 version had expired.
- Math: $10M building, cost seg finds 30% in shorter-life categories, that's $3M written off in year one.
- **The honest half:** it isn't a permanent tax break. It's an interest-free loan from the government. You pay it back when you sell, unless you 1031 into another building, which carries its own risks.

## Fact check
Accurate as stated. Nonresidential real property depreciates over 39 years. Cost segregation reclassifies components into shorter lives. 100% bonus depreciation was restored permanently by the 2025 bill after phasing down from the 2017 law. Depreciation recapture is owed on sale and a 1031 exchange defers it.

**The one thing he leaves out:** passive activity loss rules. Most investors can't deduct those losses against ordinary W-2 income unless they qualify as a real estate professional or the property is a short-term rental. Tax people will raise this in comments. A single line — "whether you can actually use those losses depends on your situation, talk to your CPA" — closes it without a tax lecture.

## YouTube description
When you hear that people in real estate make millions of dollars and pay no taxes, here's what they're actually doing.

When you buy a commercial building, the IRS says you have to depreciate it over 39 years. In reality, that building is made up of many parts, and not all of them are going to last 39 years. So what the law allows you to do is get what's called a cost segregation study. It's a report you hire a company to produce that breaks the building down into its components: HVAC systems, the parking lot striping, landscaping, flooring, cabinets. It sorts those components into categories that can be depreciated over a shorter period than 39 years.

Then there's 100% bonus depreciation, which lets you write off all of those shorter-life items in year one. The bill passed in 2025 made that permanent. In 2017 we had 100% bonus depreciation for a period of time and then it expired. Now it's here to stay.

So if you buy a building for $10 million and a cost segregation study says 30% of it is depreciable over a shorter period, that's $3 million you can write off in year one.

What you need to keep in mind is that this is not a permanent tax break. It's basically an interest free loan from the government. When you sell that building, you're going to have to pay those taxes, unless you 1031 exchange into another building, which has its own risks and challenges. This is not tax advice, but that's what people mean when they say they don't pay taxes in the real estate business.
