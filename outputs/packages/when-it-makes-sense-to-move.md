What do I wish business owners knew before they call a broker? That moving is going to take longer than they think and cost more than they expect. It's the broker's job to communicate that and set the proper expectations up front, but tenants consistently underestimate how difficult it is to move.

Usually it starts because everyone has something they don't like about their current space. But once you get out in the market, see what's actually available, and figure out what it would cost to get a space to where you want it, the picture changes.

It usually only makes sense to move if you need significantly more space, significantly less space, or if you want to be in a totally different type of building or a totally different part of town.

## EDIT NOTES
- **Put the three conditions on screen as cards.** Significantly more space, significantly less space, different building type or part of town. That's the usable part and it goes by in eight seconds.
- **He's arguing against his own interest again.** Tenant rep brokers get paid when tenants move. He's telling tenants most of them shouldn't. That's the same credibility move as talking clients out of deals, and it's worth protecting in the edit — don't cut the part where he says moving rarely makes sense.
- **Needs one number.** "Costs more than you expect" is the claim; what a move actually runs per square foot, or how many months it really takes, would make it concrete. Same receipts gap as the rest of the batch.
- **Pairs with "finding the right space is more than square footage."** That clip says understand the business before you look. This one says make sure you should be looking at all. Together they're the honest front end of a tenant rep pitch.
- **Answers a Batch 18 question** ("what do you wish tenants knew before they call a broker"), so cross it off that list.
- **Good ad candidate for the tenant side.** The viewer is a business owner who's unhappy with their space and hasn't called anyone yet, which is the exact moment before a tenant rep assignment starts.
