## YouTube description
Finding the right space for a company is a lot more nuanced than square footage, a part of town, and a budget. Every business is different. They have different needs and different motivations, and without understanding the business, you can't find the space that's going to work best for it.

Understanding a business takes more time than looking at the numbers. But if you focus only on the numbers and not on what makes that company unique and what it actually needs from its space, you're not going to get the best outcome.

## EDIT NOTES
- **This is the thinnest clip in the recent batch.** The point is right, but it's all principle and no example, so there's nothing for a viewer to remember. It needs one concrete case: two tenants with the same square footage who needed opposite spaces, or a client whose real priority turned out to be something the numbers never showed.
- **The example already exists in our files.** The 770 South Post Oak brief: parking is the number one complaint about Uptown office space, and it never shows up in a square footage, location, and budget search. If Brandon can say "a law firm needed X square feet in Uptown, but what actually decided it was where their clients park," that's the clip.
- **Pair it with the First Question clip.** His first question for a tenant is "what are the top things most important for you to have in your space?" That's this clip's argument in action. Cut them together, or post back to back.
- **Reach expectation:** tenant rep education, low organic reach. More useful as support content for 770 South Post Oak and future lease listings than as a standalone post.
